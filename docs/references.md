# Literature and dependencies

1. J. H. Conway, **An Enumeration of Knots and Links**, in *Computational Problems in Abstract Algebra* (1969), pp. 329–358. Rational tangles and tangle composition.
2. Francis Bonahon and Laurence Siebenmann, **New Geometric Splittings of Classical Knots and the Classification and Symmetries of Arborescent Knots**, revised manuscript (2016): https://dornsife.usc.edu/francis-bonahon/wp-content/uploads/sites/205/2023/06/BonSieb-compressed.pdf . Essential source for Conway splitting, weighted planar trees and topological equivalences.
3. David Futer, Efstratia Kalfagianni and Jessica S. Purcell, **Guts of Surfaces and the Colored Jones Polynomial**, §8.1 and its account of Bonahon–Siebenmann Theorem 12.28: https://faculty.cst.temple.edu/~dfuter/research/guts-cJP-book.pdf . Rotations and reversal concern **Montesinos links**, not arbitrary boundary-fixed tangles.
4. Louis H. Kauffman and Sofia Lambropoulou, **On the Classification of Rational Tangles**, *Advances in Applied Mathematics* 33 (2004), 199–237, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0311499 . Slope classification and conventions.

Before promoting a presentation-count formula to an isotopy-class statement, identify the precise theorem, orientation/boundary conventions, and exceptional cases. In particular do not transfer cyclic-link dihedral moves to marked tangles. Square-root asymptotics also need explicit analytic grammar assumptions.
