# Literature and interpretation boundaries

1. Francis Bonahon and Laurence C. Siebenmann, *New Geometric Splittings of Classical Knots and the Classification and Symmetries of Arborescent Knots* (revised manuscript, 26 January 2016):
   https://dornsife.usc.edu/francis-bonahon/wp-content/uploads/sites/205/2023/06/BonSieb-compressed.pdf
   Introduction: characterization by weighted planar trees; Chapters 11–13: classifications; ``12.2–12.7: plumbing and weighted-planar-tree moves, canonical trees and tangle calculus.
2. David Futer, Efstratia Kalfagianni and Jessica S. Purcell, *Guts of Surfaces and the Colored Jones Polynomial*, Chapter 8 `8.1 / Theorem 8.2, citing the Bonahon–Siebenmann classification:
   https://faculty.cst.temple.edu/~dfuter/research/guts-cJP-book.pdf
   Montesinos link data: total rational slope and fractional slopes up to dihedral order, with the theorem's exact hypotheses and conventions.
3. J. H. Conway, *An Enumeration of Knots and Links, and Some of Their Algebraic Properties* (1970): tangle sum/product and algebraic tangle constructions.
4. L. H. Kauffman and S. Lambropoulou, work on rational tangles, slopes and continued-fraction classification: rational tangle equivalence **with marked boundary** differs from equivalence of closures.

Before extending M0 beyond its restricted alphabet, recheck the exact theorem assumptions: possible exceptional Seifert cases, twist sign convention, ambient orientation, mirror equivalence, and markings. The software only checks **combinatorial parameter orbits**; the mathematical bridge to topology is supplied by literature, not by code.

Nothing here proves the conjectural growth law for general arborescent tangles.
