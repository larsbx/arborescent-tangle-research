# A1: Weighted planar-tree syntax and bounded branching — exact baseline

**Status:** implemented, locally tested, *combinatorial presentations only*. This is
the first step toward the Bonahon–Siebenmann plumbing calculus, **not** a
implementation of its flips, ring moves, canonical trees or isotopy test.

## Direct source and limitations

Bonahon–Siebenmann, *New Geometric Splittings of Classical Knots and the
Classification and Symmetries of Arborescent Knots*, revised Jan. 2016,
§12.4, pp. 153–156, especially Weight (W), Free Bond (F), Stick (S),
Positive (P) and Negative (N) canonicity conditions, and Theorem 12.12:

https://dornsife.usc.edu/francis-bonahon/wp-content/uploads/sites/205/2023/06/BonSieb-compressed.pdf

Our vertex model has a counterclockwise/cyclically ordered list of **bonds**
and, between successive bonds, a list of **integer angular sector weights**.
Internal bonds occur twice on two distinct vertices; free bonds occur once.
The underlying graph is checked to be a connected acyclic tree. A marked
free bond fixes the presentation root.

`rooted_plane_code` is invariant under vertex and bond renumbering and
under cyclic rotation of a vertex's incident list (with the sector list
rotated in parallel). It is a normal form **for marked weighted plane-tree
isomorphism only**, not for plumbing equivalence.

The `wfs_failures` predicates implement **strict nonexceptional**
necessary conditions:

- **W:** at most one nonzero sector at any vertex.
- **F:** every free bond is attached to a zero-weight vertex with bond
  valence at least 3.
- **S:** after removing vertices of bond valence at least 3, each connected
  remaining *stick* has nonzero weights with alternating signs; endpoint
  magnitudes are at least 2.

The small exceptional tree cases described in the manuscript are deliberately
**excluded** by this strict checker. It is not a checker for (P), (N),
the modified ring moves, flips, pairwise irreducibility, or the hypotheses
of Theorem 12.12; passing W/F/S alone does not certify a canonical tree.

## A finite, explicitly predeclared branching grammar

Fix a finite alphabet `L` of allowed leaf weights satisfying `|w|>=2`,
with `m=|L|`. Consider **rooted ordered** plane trees generated by:

- `Leaf(w)` for `w in L` (one weighted sector at a valence-one vertex);
- `Fork(A,B)` or `Fork(A,B,C)` (zero weights on all sectors);
- a **marked root free bond** at the root fork; no other free bonds.

The fork bond valence is respectively 3 or 4 including its incoming
bond, and all terminal leaf weights have magnitude at least 2. Every
nontrivial generated tree therefore passes the strict W/F/S checks.
For a root leaf alone (size zero), F fails: this is a formal base
term in the generating series, **not** an admitted positive-size tree.

The grammar has genuinely branching nodes and retains angular sectors and
planar rotations, unlike the previous horizontal-sum parenthesization model.
It does *not* assert that two distinct generated trees give nonisotopic
arborescent links; equivalence under the published plumbing moves is open.

### Theorem A1.1 — exact syntax series

Let `a_n(m)` count grammar objects with `n` internal forks, including
formal leaf base terms at `n=0`. Then

```text
F_m(x) = sum(n>=0) a_n(m) x^n
F_m(x) = m + x*(F_m(x)^2 + F_m(x)^3)
```

is an algebraic generating series over `Q(x)`. The recurrence implemented
in `syntax_counts` is the coefficient extraction from this equation.

**Proof.** The base object is one of `m` leaves. Otherwise a root fork
contributes one internal vertex and has either two or three ordered
independent child subtrees. The cases are disjoint and exhaust syntax.

### Theorem A1.2 — arity-refined integer formula

If `j` of the `n` forks are ternary, the remaining `n-j` are binary,
and the number of leaves is `n+j+1`. The exact count is

```text
a_(n,j)(m) =
    (2*n+j)! / [(n-j)! * j! * (n+j+1)!] * m^(n+j+1)

a_n(m) = sum(j=0..n) a_(n,j)(m)
```

**Proof.** An ordered plane rooted tree with counts `(n-j,j,n+j+1)`
of vertices of outdegrees `(2,3,0)` has `N=2n+j+1` vertices. The
cycle lemma / preorder Łukasiewicz-word condition picks exactly one
valid rooted tree among the `N` cyclic shifts of each such word, giving
`(1/N) * N! / [(n-j)!j!(n+j+1)!]`. Each of the `n+j+1` leaves has `m`
independent labels. This is an **exact integer** formula, and a second
implementation checks the recursive counts.

## Next topological bridge (A2)

1. Add the precise Bonahon–Siebenmann moves (including boundary, flips,
   ring, and local arithmetic) as individually sourced, typed rewrites;
   attach section/page and hypothesis to each move.
2. Prove *soundness* of each rewrite against the plumbing construction;
   don't infer it merely from equal combinatorial encodings.
3. Encode full (W), (F), (S), (P)/(N) and exceptional trees with
   correct valence/free-bond conventions.
4. Test root/boundary marking loss, orientation, mirror, positive/negative
   canonicity and exceptional decomposition samples.
5. Only then enumerate true canonical-tree orbits and compare to known
   rational/Montesinos-link classification and independent diagram data.

A1 is deliberately smaller than A2. It is a precise, independently
checkable syntactic platform on which the full equivalence calculus can
subsequently be built.
